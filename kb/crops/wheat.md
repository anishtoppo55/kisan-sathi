# Gehun / Wheat (गेहूँ)
Wheat is grown in Chhattisgarh in irrigated fields after paddy. Chhattisgarh is in the Central Zone (CZ) of ICAR-IIWBR. Sow in November for timely sowing. Do not grow varieties recommended for other zones, as they can be more prone to disease.

## Sowing time and seed rate (buvai ka samay aur beej dar)
<!-- stage: sowing; topic: sowing, variety -->
- Timely sowing is in November. Sowing after December 25 is very late and reduces yield.
- Late sown wheat: use about 125 kg seed per hectare (50 kg per acre), keep row spacing at about 17.5 cm and sow shallow.
- Choose a variety suited to your sowing date; for very late sowing beyond December 25, IIWBR lists HD 3271, HI 1621 and HD 2851 for all zones. Ask your KVK for the best timely-sown variety for your district.
- Seed treatment against loose smut: carboxin 75% WP at 2–2.5 g per kg seed, or tebuconazole 2% DS at 0.2 kg per 10 kg seed.

## Fertilizer (khaad)
<!-- stage: sowing, vegetative; topic: fertilizer -->
- IIWBR dose for late sown irrigated wheat in the Central Zone: 90:60:40 kg N:P:K per hectare.
- Give one third of the nitrogen and all phosphorus and potash at sowing; give the remaining nitrogen in two equal splits at the first and second irrigation.
- Finish nitrogen application by 40–45 days after sowing. Apply urea just before irrigation.
- If the crop is yellow, do not add extra urea, and avoid urea in foggy or cloudy weather.
- Best: follow the doses on your Soil Health Card.

## Irrigation (sinchai)
<!-- stage: sowing, vegetative, flowering, maturity; topic: irrigation -->
- First irrigation 20–25 days after sowing, at crown root initiation (CRI, taaj jad avastha). This is the most important irrigation.
- Other critical stages: tillering, jointing, flowering and grain filling (milk and dough stage). If water is limited, never miss CRI, then flowering and grain filling.
- Check the weather forecast; skip irrigation if rain is forecast, to avoid waterlogging.
- Irrigate lightly in strong wind to prevent lodging (fasal girna) after heading.

## Weed control (kharpatwar)
<!-- stage: vegetative; topic: weed -->
- Narrow leaf weeds (gulli danda / kanki): clodinafop 15 WP at 160 g per acre, or pinoxaden 5 EC at 400 ml per acre.
- Broad leaf weeds: 2,4-D at 500 ml per acre, or metsulfuron 20 WP at 8 g per acre, or carfentrazone 40 DF at 20 g per acre.
- Both types: sulfosulfuron 75 WG at 13.5 g per acre in 120–150 litres of water, before the first irrigation or 10–15 days after it.
- Spray only in clear weather (no rain, fog or dew).

## Yellow rust and other rusts (peela ratua, geru rog)
<!-- stage: vegetative, flowering; topic: disease -->
- Symptom: yellow powdery stripes on leaves (yellow rust); brown round pustules (brown rust). Yellowing can also be from other causes, so confirm with a KVK or wheat expert.
- Avoid excess nitrogen.
- Control: propiconazole 25% EC at 200 ml in 200 litres of water per acre, or tebuconazole 25% EC at 200 ml in 200 litres of water per acre.

## Termites (deemak)
<!-- stage: sowing, vegetative; topic: pest -->
- Seed treatment: thiamethoxam 30% FS at 1.32 kg per 40 kg seed, or chlorpyrifos 20% EC at 3–4 ml per kg seed.

## Heat stress at grain filling (garmi se nuksaan)
<!-- stage: flowering, maturity; topic: weather -->
- High temperatures (above about 35°C) during flowering and grain filling shrivel the grain. Late sowing increases this risk.
- When heat is forecast at this stage, give a light irrigation, preferably in the evening, to keep the soil moist.
