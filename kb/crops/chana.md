# Chana / Gram (चना)
Chana is the main rabi pulse of Chhattisgarh. It is sown after the kharif harvest in October–November, mostly on residual moisture, and harvested in February–March. It needs little water and fixes nitrogen in the soil.

## Sowing and varieties (buvai aur kism)
<!-- stage: sowing; topic: variety, sowing -->
- Sow on time (October to mid-November); early and timely sowing with early maturing varieties reduces pod borer damage.
- Wilt-tolerant varieties listed by NCIPM include JG 11, JG 12, JG 14, JG 16, JG 315, JG 322, JG 63, JG 74, JAKI 9218, Vijay, Vishal, Rajas and others. Ask your KVK which of these is available and suits your area.
- Do not grow chana in fields with severe wilt or dry root rot for at least three years; rotate with a non-legume crop.
- Mix about 5 g of rabi sorghum (jowar) or sunflower seed with the chana seed so birds that eat caterpillars are attracted.

## Seed treatment (beej upchar)
<!-- stage: sowing; topic: disease, pest -->
- Treat seed with Trichoderma viride at 4–10 g per kg of seed against wilt and root rot.
- In wilt or root rot affected fields, mix Trichoderma viride at 5 kg per hectare with about 100 kg well-decomposed FYM (gobar khaad) and apply to moist soil.
- Against termites (deemak), seed treatment with chlorpyriphos 20 EC at 15–30 ml per kg of seed.

## Wilt and root rot (ukhta / ukheda rog)
<!-- stage: sowing, vegetative, flowering; topic: disease -->
- Symptom: plants droop and dry in patches although the soil is moist; split stem near the base shows brown-black streaks inside.
- ETL: 5–10% plants infested.
- Prevention is the main control: resistant varieties, Trichoderma seed treatment, crop rotation and avoiding moisture stress.

## Irrigation (sinchai)
<!-- stage: vegetative, flowering; topic: irrigation -->
- Chana needs little water. Where irrigation is available, one light irrigation at branching (about 40–45 days) and, if needed, one at pod formation is usually enough.
- Avoid irrigation at full flowering and avoid waterlogging; excess water causes flower drop, too much vegetative growth and root diseases.
- Do not irrigate if rain is forecast.
- In deep black soils with excessive growth, nipping the shoot tips (detopping) at about 30 days after sowing is recommended.

## Pod borer (fali chhedak, ilii)
<!-- stage: flowering, maturity; topic: pest -->
- Symptom: green caterpillars eat leaves and then bore into pods, eating the seeds.
- Monitor with pheromone traps; fix bird perches (T-shaped sticks) at about 20 per hectare.
- ETL: 5–8 eggs or 2 small larvae per 10 plants, or one grown larva (more than 1 cm) per 10 plants or per metre of row.
- First option: neem-based azadirachtin 0.03% at 2.5–5 litre per hectare or NSKE 5% at the pre-flowering stage; HaNPV at 250 LE per hectare in the evening when the first young larvae appear; or Bt (Bacillus thuringiensis) at 1 kg per hectare.
- After ETL, chemical options: emamectin benzoate 5% SG at 220 g per hectare, or novaluron 10% EC at 700 ml per hectare, or quinalphos 25 EC at 1 litre per hectare.

## Frost and cold (paala)
<!-- stage: flowering, maturity; topic: weather -->
- Frost on clear, calm winter nights can damage flowers and young pods.
- When frost is forecast, give a light irrigation in the evening and, where practical, make smoke on the windward side of the field at night.

## Harvest (katai)
<!-- stage: maturity, post-harvest; topic: harvest -->
- Harvest when leaves turn reddish-brown and shed and pods are dry. Dry the plants in the sun and thresh; store seed with low moisture in clean bags.
- After harvest, deep ploughing and destroying stubble reduces wilt and pests in the next season.
