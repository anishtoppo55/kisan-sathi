# Teora / Lakhdi / Lathyrus (तिवड़ा / लाखड़ी)
Teora (also called tiwda, lakhdi or khesari) is a hardy rabi pulse that grows on residual moisture after paddy, with almost no irrigation. In Chhattisgarh it is mostly grown by the utera (paira) method: seed is broadcast into the standing paddy crop before harvest. The crop needs about 15–25°C from sowing to harvest.

## Utera method (utera / paira vidhi)
<!-- stage: sowing; topic: sowing -->
- No tillage is needed under the utera system.
- Broadcast the teora seed evenly into the standing paddy about 1–2 weeks before paddy harvest, when the field is still moist but not flooded (the soil should be soft, like wet dough).
- Harvest the paddy carefully, cutting high so the young teora seedlings are not damaged.
- Utera saves the cost and time of ploughing and uses the moisture left after paddy, so it is useful where irrigation is not available.

## Seed rate and varieties (beej dar aur kism)
<!-- stage: sowing; topic: variety, sowing -->
- Utera sowing needs a higher seed rate than line sowing because some seed does not germinate. Field studies in Chhattisgarh found about 60 kg per hectare gave a good crop stand; confirm the rate for your variety with the KVK.
- Use improved low-ODAP varieties such as Ratan, Prateek and Mahateora. Low ODAP means lower levels of the natural toxin, so the grain is safer to eat.
- In Chhattisgarh plains, studies found sowing around late November gave higher yield than later sowing; delayed sowing reduced yield.

## Water and nutrients (paani aur khaad)
<!-- stage: vegetative, flowering; topic: irrigation, fertilizer -->
- Teora usually grows without irrigation on residual moisture. If the soil becomes very dry before flowering and water is available, one light irrigation helps.
- Avoid waterlogging.
- Being a pulse, it fixes nitrogen; no or very little nitrogen fertilizer is needed. Phosphorus helps where the soil is poor; follow your Soil Health Card.

## Pests and diseases (keet aur rog)
<!-- stage: vegetative, flowering; topic: pest, disease -->
- Watch for aphids (maahu) on shoot tips and for powdery mildew (white powder on leaves) in cool, humid weather.
- Ask the KVK or RAEO before spraying anything; follow the label dose.

## Harvest and use (katai aur upyog)
<!-- stage: maturity, post-harvest; topic: harvest -->
- Harvest when most pods are dry and brown, usually February–March.
- Teora is also a good green fodder and improves the soil for the next paddy crop.
- Eat teora dal in moderation as part of a mixed diet; low-ODAP varieties are safer.
