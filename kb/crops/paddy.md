# Dhaan / Paddy (धान)
Paddy is the main kharif crop of Chhattisgarh ("dhaan ka katora"). It is transplanted or direct-seeded in June–July and harvested in October–November. Common varieties include MTU-1010, Swarna, and IGKV Raipur varieties such as Rajeshwari, Indira Sona, Indira Maheshwari and Chhattisgarh Zinc Rice-1. Choose a variety suited to your land (bahra/low land or tikra/upland) on the advice of your RAEO or KVK.

## Integrated pest management basics (IPM, keet niyantran ke niyam)
<!-- stage: all; topic: pest -->
- Check the field every week; walk diagonally and look at 20 hills.
- Spray chemicals only when a pest crosses its Economic Threshold Level (ETL, aarthik kshati star). Below ETL, natural enemies like spiders, ladybird beetles and dragonflies keep pests in check.
- Use pheromone traps for stem borer monitoring and light traps where possible.
- Avoid excess nitrogen (urea); it increases brown planthopper, blast and bacterial leaf blight.
- Spray in clear weather, not before rain, and use the exact product and dose on the label or as advised by the KVK.

## Seed treatment and nursery (beej upchar aur nursery)
<!-- stage: sowing; topic: disease, pest -->
- Seed treatment for seed- and soil-borne diseases: carbendazim 50% WP at 2 g per kg seed, or Trichoderma / Pseudomonas as a bio-agent.
- Nursery ETL: yellow stem borer 1 egg mass per square metre.
- Nursery blast: spray tricyclazole 75% WP at 300–400 g per hectare, or isoprothiolane 40% EC at 750 ml per hectare.

## Stem borer (tana chhedak, dead heart, safed baali)
<!-- stage: vegetative, flowering; topic: pest -->
- Symptom: central shoot dries ("dead heart") at tillering; empty white panicles ("white ear / safed baali") at heading.
- ETL (tillering): 2 egg masses per square metre, or 10% dead hearts, or 1 moth per square metre, or 25 moths per pheromone trap per week.
- Control after ETL: cartap hydrochloride 4% granules at 18.75 kg per hectare, or cartap hydrochloride 50% SP at 1 kg per hectare.
- Remove and destroy egg masses; after harvest, plough stubble to kill hibernating larvae.

## Brown planthopper, BPH (bhura maahu, maahu)
<!-- stage: vegetative, flowering; topic: pest -->
- Symptom: hoppers at the base of plants; round patches of dried, burnt-looking plants ("hopper burn").
- ETL: 10–15 hoppers per hill at tillering; 15–20 hoppers per hill from panicle initiation to booting.
- Drain standing water for a few days and avoid extra urea.
- Control after ETL: imidacloprid 30.5% SC at 60–75 ml per hectare, or buprofezin 25% SC at 800 ml per hectare, or ethofenprox 10% EC at 500–750 ml per hectare. Direct the spray to the base of the plants.

## Leaf folder (patti lapetak)
<!-- stage: vegetative, flowering; topic: pest -->
- Symptom: leaves folded lengthwise with white streaks where the larva scrapes the leaf.
- ETL: 2 fully damaged leaves with larva per hill.
- Control after ETL: cartap hydrochloride 50% SP at 1 kg per hectare.

## Gundhi bug (gandhi bug, gundhi keet)
<!-- stage: flowering, maturity; topic: pest -->
- Symptom: bad smell in the field; grains become empty or chaffy at the milky stage.
- ETL: 2 bugs per hill from flowering to milky grain stage.
- Keep bunds and field edges free of grassy weeds, where the bug survives.

## Blast (jhulsa / neck blast, gardan tod)
<!-- stage: vegetative, flowering; topic: disease -->
- Symptom: spindle-shaped (eye-shaped) spots with grey centres on leaves; neck of the panicle turns black and breaks.
- ETL: 3–5 lesions per leaf (leaf blast); 2–5 neck-infected plants per square metre (neck blast).
- Control: tricyclazole 75% WP at 300–400 g per hectare, or isoprothiolane 40% EC at 750 ml per hectare. Apply nitrogen only in small split doses.

## Sheath blight (sheath jhulsa)
<!-- stage: vegetative, flowering; topic: disease -->
- Symptom: oval greenish-grey patches on the leaf sheath near the water line that join up.
- ETL: lesions of 5–6 mm and 2–3 infected plants per square metre.
- Control: hexaconazole 5% EC at 1 litre per hectare, or propiconazole 25% EC at 750 ml per hectare, or validamycin 3% L at 2 litre per hectare.

## Bacterial leaf blight, BLB (jhulsa rog)
<!-- stage: vegetative, flowering; topic: disease -->
- Symptom: yellow to straw-coloured wavy stripes from the leaf tip down the edges.
- ETL: 2–3 infected leaves per square metre.
- Reduce nitrogen; apply only small split doses. Spray streptomycin sulphate 9% + tetracycline hydrochloride 1% SP at 100–150 ppm if advised by the KVK.

## Harvest (katai)
<!-- stage: maturity; topic: harvest -->
- Harvest when about 80–85% of the grains in the panicle turn golden yellow and grain is hard. Late harvest causes grain shattering and breakage.
- Drain the field 7–10 days before harvest so the soil dries.
- For teora/lakhdi or other utera crops, broadcast the seed into the standing paddy before harvest while the soil is still moist (see the teora guide).

## Drying and storage (sukhana aur bhandaran)
<!-- stage: post-harvest; topic: harvest, storage -->
- Dry the grain in the sun on a clean surface until moisture is about 12–14% (grain cracks sharply when bitten) before storage or sale.
- Clean the grain of chaff and weeds; well-dried, clean paddy gets the full price at the society.
- Store in clean, dry gunny bags on wooden planks, away from walls; keep the store dry and protect it from rats.
- After harvest, plough the stubble instead of burning it; burning kills soil life and natural enemies.
